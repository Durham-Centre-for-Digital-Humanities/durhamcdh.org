Submit a Project for Archiving

We preserve student and researcher digital humanities projects as permanent, open, static archives. Read the guidelines below, then complete the submission form.

Impact & Sustainability

A digital project that disappears the moment your dissertation is submitted leaves no trace in the scholarly record. Archiving your work means it can be cited, reused, and built upon by future researchers — making your contribution part of a living intellectual commons rather than a private experiment.

Funders — including AHRC, the ERC, and Horizon Europe — increasingly require that research outputs remain publicly accessible for years or decades. Submitting your project to a maintained institutional archive satisfies open-access obligations and demonstrates long-term research impact.

Static & Server-Free by Design

Most student digital projects rely on a live database and application server. That means the project dies the moment the server is switched off, the hosting subscription lapses, or a dependency breaks.

We work with you to export your database to flat JSON files and rebuild the front end to run entirely in the browser — no PHP, no Python, no server process. A static site has no attack surface, no credentials to leak, no runtime to patch, and no hosting costs beyond simple file storage. It will still work in twenty years.

Open Data

Where possible we publish your project data as downloadable JSON alongside the archived interface. Open data means other researchers can load your dataset into their own tools, run new analyses, and compare findings without reverse-engineering a live application.

We encourage contributors to apply an open licence (CC BY 4.0 is the default unless your data contains sensitive or third-party material). We will advise you on what can be released openly and help you prepare a clear data statement.

How the Process Works

After you submit the form below, we will make contact within five working days to discuss the project and agree a plan.

  1. 01

    Submit the form

    Fill in all the fields below — project details, technology stack, data sources, and your contact information.

  2. 02

    Initial consultation

    We review the submission and arrange a short call or email exchange to understand the project architecture and data.

  3. 03

    Data export & rebuild

    We help you export the database to JSON and adapt or replace the front end so it runs without a server or database connection.

  4. 04

    Publish & cite

    The archive goes live under a permanent URL at dcdh.org/projects. We provide a citation and DOI where applicable.

Before you submit

  • ✓ You have access to the project source code or can obtain it
  • ✓ You are a Durham student, postgraduate, or member of staff
  • ✓ The project has genuine humanities or interdisciplinary content
  • ✓ You are willing to provide or help create a JSON export of your data
  • ✓ You have considered whether third-party data can be openly licensed
