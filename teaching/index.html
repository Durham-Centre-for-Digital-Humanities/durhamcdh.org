Teaching

Develop your digital and computational skills through teaching that brings artificial intelligence and data science into conversation with the humanities.

Featured programme

MSc in Applied Artificial Intelligence and Data Science in Digital Humanities

Are you a student or recent graduate in the humanities who would like to develop quantitative and computational skills in order to stand out in today's hyper-competitive job market?

Would you like to understand how AI works and how to make it work for you?

Durham's MSc in Applied Artificial Intelligence and Data Science in Digital Humanities was created to teach advanced skills in AI and data science to humanities graduates, complementing the high-level qualitative skills you will have acquired from your undergraduate degree.

The programme is designed for complete beginners and requires no prior knowledge of, or qualification in, mathematics or programming. Starting from scratch, you will learn statistical modelling in R and computer programming in Python, progressing to advanced topics including machine learning, neural networks and AI. The digital humanities element builds on what you studied as an undergraduate, whether that was languages, literature, history, philosophy, music, visual culture, or theology and religion. This part of the programme will introduce you to the large-scale computational analysis of cultural artefacts: texts, objects, images and sounds.

Graduates of the programme have gone on to work as data analysts, programmers, management consultants and management trainees at a range of national and international firms. Employers increasingly want people who can collect, analyse and present numerical data alongside the qualitative skills a humanities education develops, and that is exactly the combination this programme offers.

The world we live in is changing rapidly. AI has the potential to eliminate some careers while making others vastly more productive, and a working knowledge of computing and statistics is now part of being a critical, well-informed citizen. Learning to code is far from obsolete: with today's AI tools, the distance between absolute beginner and effective programmer is much shorter than it used to be. Beyond enhancing your CV, the programme as a whole will broaden your intellectual horizons and enable you to navigate and critique our data-driven digital culture more effectively.
