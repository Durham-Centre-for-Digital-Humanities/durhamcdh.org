Biography

I am a postdoctoral researcher in Archaeology at Durham University, specialising in computational and quantitative approaches to the study of past human societies. My work integrates archaeological theory with agent-based modelling, Bayesian methods, machine learning, and complex-systems approaches. I have research experience spanning Prehistory and the first millennium CE across the Mediterranean, the Andes, and South America.

Research Interest

I am interested in understanding how human societies adapt to environmental change, organise resources, and respond to social and ecological constraints. I use computational approaches to develop and test explanations of past human behaviour, with particular interests in socioecological systems, subsistence, mobility, demography, and human–environment interactions. I am also interested in teaching quantitative and computational methods in archaeology.

Digital Humanities Interest

I am interested in using digital methods to move beyond the description of archaeological patterns towards the formalisation and testing of explanations. I am particularly interested in developing models that integrate archaeological evidence with environmental and social data, as well as exploring emerging applications of artificial intelligence and digital methods in archaeological research and teaching.

Digital Literacy

I have advanced experience applying computational and quantitative methods to archaeological research, including agent-based modelling, Bayesian networks, causal modelling, supervised machine learning, statistical analysis, and simulation. I am experienced in translating archaeological theory into formal computational models, implementing and testing models, analysing outputs, and sharing reproducible research.
